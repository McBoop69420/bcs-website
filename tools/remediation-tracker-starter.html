The columns that matter and the habits that keep findings moving toward closure. Rebuild this in a spreadsheet, or print it to run a review.

The fields that matter

Most trackers decay because they record status without driving action. These are the columns worth keeping — everything else is optional.

What each field is for

  • Source — exam, audit, risk assessment, pen test, or internal. Ties the item back to why it exists.
  • Risk — a simple High / Medium / Low, so the list can be sorted by what matters most.
  • Owner — a named person, never a team. One name per finding.
  • Due — a real date. "Ongoing" is where findings go to die.
  • Next action — the single concrete step that happens next, not a status adjective.

Habits that keep it alive

  • A short standing review (monthly is plenty) where each open item gets a next action and a date.
  • Overdue items are escalated, not silently rolled forward.
  • Closed items keep their evidence (what changed, when, who verified) for the exam folder.
  • New findings land here the day they're identified, not at the next review.
A tracker is a management tool, not a filing cabinet. If an item hasn't moved in two reviews, that's the signal to escalate, re-scope, or formally accept the risk — not to copy the status forward again.
