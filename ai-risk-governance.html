Every business has a different AI risk profile. The goal isn't to solve every possible AI risk.

Where AI creates risk

In how your employees work, and in how attackers target your business.

Shadow AI

Employees adopt AI tools on their own to move faster, often outside any approval process and without anyone tracking what data those tools see.

Sensitive data exposure

Customer records, financial details, contracts, and internal plans get pasted into AI platforms with unclear data handling and retention.

AI-generated phishing

Convincing, well-written, targeted attacks are now available to anyone, and much harder to catch by instinct alone.

Deepfake fraud

Synthetic voice and video are used to impersonate executives and authorize payments or access that would normally be verified in person.

Third-party AI

The software you already use is adding AI features, which can quietly change what happens to your data once it leaves your systems.

New governance obligations

Regulators, insurers, and customers are starting to ask how you govern AI use, before most SMBs have an answer ready.

You don't need to become an AI security expert. You need to know where AI is creating risk, which risks matter, and what to do about them.
