AI Is Changing Where Your Business Is Exposed to Risk

Not by replacing your cybersecurity problems — by adding new ones on top of them. Here’s how to find out which ones actually apply to you.

Where AI Is Creating Risk

AI is changing how your employees work, and how attackers target your business.

Shadow AI

Employees adopt AI tools on their own to move faster, often outside of any approval process and without anyone tracking what data those tools see.

Sensitive data exposure

Customer records, financial details, contracts, and internal plans get pasted into AI platforms with unclear data handling and retention practices.

AI-generated phishing and social engineering

AI makes convincing, well-written, targeted attacks available to anyone — and much harder for employees to catch by instinct alone.

Deepfake impersonation and fraud

Synthetic voice and video are being used to impersonate executives and authorize payments or access that would normally be verified in person.

AI vendors and third-party data risk

The software you already use is adding AI features, which can quietly change what happens to your data once it leaves your systems.

New governance and compliance obligations

Regulators, insurers, and customers are starting to ask how you govern AI use — before most SMBs have an answer ready.

You don’t need to become an AI security expert. You need a practical way to understand and manage your risk.

The Real Questions

Every business has a different AI risk profile.

The goal isn’t to solve every possible AI risk. It’s to answer the questions that actually determine yours.

  • How is AI actually being used in our organization?
  • What data is being exposed to AI tools?
  • Which risks actually apply to us?
  • Which risks matter most?
  • Who is responsible for AI governance?
  • What controls already exist?
  • Where are the real gaps?
  • What should we do first?
