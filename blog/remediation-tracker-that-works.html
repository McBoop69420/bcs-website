Building a Remediation Tracker That Actually Gets Used

Published September 22, 2026. Written for all SMBs.

Why Trackers Go Stale

Every assessment, audit, and pen test produces a list of findings, and most of those lists end up in a spreadsheet. Six months later, half the rows say “in progress,” nobody remembers what the notes mean, and the evidence that a fix was made lives in someone's sent folder.

The tool is rarely the problem. A spreadsheet works fine. Trackers fail because they don't clearly connect each finding to an owner, a deadline, and proof of completion, so nobody can tell at a glance what's actually stuck.

The Fields That Matter

  • Source: where the finding came from, such as an exam, audit, assessment, incident, vendor review, or internal testing. Findings from regulators usually carry firmer deadlines.
  • Risk statement: one sentence on what could happen if it stays open. This is what lets leadership prioritize.
  • Owner: a single named person responsible for the work. Not a team, not a department.
  • Target date: realistic, tied to risk, and changed only with a recorded reason.
  • Closure evidence: what will prove it's done, decided up front. A screenshot, a config export, a signed policy, a ticket.
  • Status: a short, fixed set of labels such as Open, In Progress, Overdue, Risk Accepted, and Closed.

Habits That Keep It Alive

Review it on a schedule

A monthly fifteen-minute review with owners does more than any tool. Go through anything overdue or due in the next thirty days, and nothing else.

Attach evidence when you close

Don't close a row until the evidence is attached or linked. Closing first and gathering proof later almost always means gathering it during the next exam, under pressure.

Make risk acceptance a real status

Some findings won't be fixed, and that can be the right call. Record who accepted the risk and when it gets revisited, rather than leaving the row open forever.

Make It Readable for Leadership

Leadership shouldn't have to read every row. A short summary with counts by status, the oldest open items, and anything high risk that's overdue is enough to support real decisions. If the tracker can't produce that summary in a few minutes, it's carrying too much detail in the wrong places.
