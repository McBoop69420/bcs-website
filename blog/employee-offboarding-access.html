Employee Offboarding: Closing the Access Gap When People Leave

Published November 3, 2026. Written for all SMBs.

The Gap Nobody Owns

When someone leaves, the parts everyone remembers happen fast: the laptop comes back, the email goes to an out-of-office, and the farewell card goes around. The part that gets missed is quieter. It's the cloud app a department signed up for on its own, the shared login nobody wanted to change, the VPN certificate still sitting on a personal phone. Each of those is a working door into your environment that belongs to someone who no longer works for you.

That gap exists because offboarding usually lives in three places at once. HR knows the person left, IT controls most of the accounts, and the managers know the odd tools their teams actually use. When no single checklist pulls those three together, access lingers by default. Attackers know this, and so do examiners, which is why dormant and orphaned accounts show up so often in both breach reports and exam findings.

What Actually Gets Missed

Disabling the primary network account and email is the easy 80 percent. The access that lingers is almost always in the same handful of blind spots.

Accounts outside the directory

Anything that doesn't authenticate through your central identity system has to be closed by hand: standalone SaaS tools a team bought on a credit card, the banking and payroll portals, the domain registrar, social media, and the marketing platforms. These rarely appear on IT's radar until they're needed.

Shared and service credentials

If the person knew a shared password, disabling their named account changes nothing. The Wi-Fi key, the shared mailbox, the admin login three people use, all of it is still valid. Offboarding a person who held a shared credential means rotating that credential, not just closing an account.

Access that isn't a login

Building badges, alarm codes, VPN certificates and tokens, SSH keys, API keys they generated, and mobile devices enrolled in your systems all grant access without a username prompt. So does mail that quietly forwards to a personal address, which is a common way a departing employee keeps seeing internal traffic.

The elevated roles

Administrators and anyone with access to money or customer data deserve same-day attention and a second person watching. These are the accounts where a delay does the most damage, and the ones an examiner will ask about first.

A Checklist That Runs the Same Every Time

The goal isn't a longer process, it's a repeatable one that doesn't depend on who happens to handle it. A workable version has four parts.

  • A trigger that reaches IT directly. HR's notice of a departure should open the offboarding task automatically, not travel by word of mouth. For a planned exit, schedule the cutoff for the last working hour. For a termination, the account disable should happen during the conversation, not after it.
  • A master list of systems. Keep a living inventory of every system that grants access, maintained alongside your vendor list. The checklist walks that inventory the same way each time so nothing depends on memory.
  • Named owners for the odd corners. IT closes the directory accounts. The person's manager confirms the team-specific tools. Facilities handles badges and codes. Each line has an owner so none of it falls through the middle.
  • Mailbox and file handling decided up front. Agree in advance how long a mailbox stays accessible to a manager and when it's removed, so retention is a policy rather than a last-minute judgment call.

Make It Provable

A closed account you can't prove you closed is a finding waiting to happen. The offboarding record should show who left, when access was removed, who removed it, and the systems covered. A completed checklist with dates and initials is enough, and it turns offboarding into evidence you can hand to an auditor instead of a story you have to reconstruct later.

Pair the checklist with a periodic look back. A quarterly access review is where you catch the account that slipped past a rushed exit, before it sits dormant for a year. Offboarding closes the door on the way out; the review confirms it stayed shut.
